## 8. Literature Review & Theoretical Framework

The strategic design outlined in this document is grounded in established strategic management literature and contemporary market dynamics. A review of current academic and industry literature informs the choice of frameworks utilized in this strategy.

### 8.1 Dynamic Capabilities Theory
As articulated by Teece, Pisano, and Shuen (1997), *Dynamic Capabilities* refer to the firm's ability to integrate, build, and reconfigure internal and external competences to address rapidly changing environments. The strategy's emphasis on cloud migration and agile R&D directly applies this theory, ensuring the firm is not merely relying on existing core competencies but is actively adapting its resource base to the shifting digital landscape.

### 8.2 Blue Ocean Strategy
Kim and Mauborgne (2005) propose that companies can succeed not by battling competitors in overcrowded industries ("Red Oceans"), but by creating uncontested market space ("Blue Oceans"). The decision to pivot toward emerging markets (Southeast Asia and Latin America) and the Gen-Z demographic is a direct application of Blue Ocean logic—demanding new demand and making the competition irrelevant by targeting previously underserved segments.

### 8.3 Resource-Based View (RBV)
The RBV (Barney, 1991) suggests that firms possess resources (tangible and intangible) that are heterogeneous and immobile. If these resources are valuable, rare, inimitable, and non-substitutable (VRIN), they yield sustained competitive advantage. This strategy leverages the firm's proprietary data analytics capabilities (a VRIN resource) to drive the AI-driven supply chain optimization, ensuring that the operational improvements cannot be easily replicated by competitors.

### 8.4 Agile Methodology in Product Development
The literature on agile product development (Sutherland & Schwaber, 1999) emphasizes iterative progress, short delivery cycles, and continuous feedback. The strategy’s reliance on an MVP for the emerging market and the agile R&D lab reflects this methodology, minimizing upfront capital risk and allowing for rapid pivoting based on real-world user data.
